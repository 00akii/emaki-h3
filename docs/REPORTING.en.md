# Reporting Channel — About Outputs Made With Emaki H3

**If you have found a video made with Emaki H3 that infringes someone's rights, or that is being used in a way it should not be, this page is how you tell us.**

**You do not need to be a user of Emaki H3 to report something.** Reports from people who simply came across the content are welcome.

## What to report

- **Infringement of a third party's rights** — copyright, trademark, likeness or publicity rights, or any other right
- **Use prohibited by the MiniMax H3 Acceptable Use Policy** (Exhibit A of the license)
- **Use that violates applicable law**
- **Harmful content involving minors**

**You do not need to be certain.** "Is this a problem?" is a perfectly good report. Being asked a question we cannot answer is a much smaller problem than not being told at all.

**⚠ For bugs and feature requests about the application itself, please use a normal Issue instead.**

## Where to report

**There are two routes. Choose whichever suits the report.**

### 1. If the report can be public → **Issues**

Open an Issue on the repository. The exchange will be publicly visible.

### 2. If the report should not be public → **private report form**

Use the **Security** tab on the repository to report privately. **Only you and we can read it.**

> **⚠ The button is labelled "Report a vulnerability".**
> That wording comes from GitHub and we cannot change it. **Please use it for rights and misuse reports as well.**
> Do not hold back because your report is not a security vulnerability.

**It does not matter whether you are the rights holder, a representative, or an unrelated third party.** You do not have to give us your name — but note that **without a way to reach you, we cannot ask follow-up questions.**

> **⚠ Both routes require a GitHub account.**
>
> Issues and the private report form are both GitHub features. **An account is free to create** ([github.com](https://github.com)).
>
> **We have not been able to provide a route that works without one.** That is a limitation on our side. **We are aware that this wall is highest for exactly the people most likely to need the channel — someone whose face or artwork was used, who has no reason to be on GitHub at all.**

### Helpful things to include

- **Where the content is** (URL, platform, filename)
- **What you believe the problem is** (which right, or which provision)
- **Your relationship to it** (rights holder / representative / third party) — optional
- **How to reach you** — optional, but it helps

## What happens after you report

1. **We will acknowledge your report within a few days.**
   *(This is a receipt so that nothing gets lost — not a conclusion.)*
2. **We review it,** and may come back with questions.
3. **We act within what we actually control,** and tell you what we did — **or why we could not.**

**⚠ We do not promise a specific response time.** Publishing a number we cannot keep would be worse than publishing none. **If you have not heard back, please chase us.**

## What we can and cannot do

**This is the most important section on this page.**

| Subject | What we can do |
|---|---|
| **Videos we published ourselves** (note, YouTube, pixiv, X, etc.) | **We can remove them** |
| **The contents of this repository** | **We can change or remove them** |
| **Distribution of the application** | We can stop it. **But copies already distributed under the MIT license cannot be recalled by us** |
| **Anything generated on someone else's computer** | **We cannot reach it** |
| **A report from someone without a GitHub account** | **We have no route for it** (as above, both routes require an account) |

**Emaki H3 runs entirely on the user's own machine.** Nothing passes through a server of ours. **We therefore do not know what anyone generates, and we have no technical means of stopping it.**

**This is not a disclaimer — it is a description of the boundary.** If a report falls outside what we can reach, **we will say so, and where we can, point you to somewhere that can act** (the platform's own reporting channel, MiniMax's contact address, and so on).

## Why this channel exists

Section **V.5** of the MiniMax H3 Community License requires a party that makes available to third parties a product, service, or hosted service capable of generating Outputs with H3 to implement safeguards, to **maintain a reasonably accessible mechanism for reporting suspected violations**, and to investigate and act on reports it receives.

**How far that provision reaches a locally-run tool like Emaki H3 is not something the available documents agree on.** See [About the License](./LICENSE-NOTES.en.md) for what each source says.

**We do not have what we would need to decide that, so we have not decided it.**

**What we did instead was take the widest reading.** Not "this might apply to us" but **"act as though it does."** This channel is the result.

**⚠ But that is as far as we got.** The clause also calls for **technical safeguards**, and **Emaki H3 has none.**
It is open source that runs on the user's own machine, so **anything we put in, a user can take out.** Rather than ship something we could call a filter and have that claim turn false the moment someone switches it off, **we chose to say there is none.**
**— This is of a piece with the table above.**

**⚠ We are not claiming that this satisfies V.5.** That is not ours to determine. **We are describing what we do.**
